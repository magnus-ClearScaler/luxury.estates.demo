# Aman on Instagram (@aman): visual language and a photo rulebook for the Riviera site

Observed 2026-10-09 from a logged-in desktop view of instagram.com/aman (read only, no interaction beyond scrolling and paging one carousel). About 36 recent grid posts sampled, caption text pulled from image alt attributes, post dates decoded from shortcodes, one 7-slide carousel (Aman Sveti Stefan, 2026-10-02) paged through slide by slide.

## 1. Account facts

- 3,979 posts, 1.2m followers, follows 55.
- Bio: one sentence about "a place of peace", then the count of hotels and countries, then a link-in-bio. No emoji, no offer, no booking link in the bio text.
- Avatar: the AMAN wordmark (thin, widely tracked caps, macron over the first A) in charcoal on a warm off-white disc. No icon or monogram.
- Highlights: Rituals of Joy, Wonders, Omotenashi, Elements, Amangati, Nature, Wellness. Covers are plain colour fields (dusk blue, terracotta, ochre, mist grey, rose) with no text or icons. Highlights are named by **ideas and feelings**, never by product ("Rooms", "Offers", "Book").
- Cadence: about 33 posts between 17 Aug and 8 Oct 2026, so 5 to 6 posts a week, nearly daily. Seasonal campaigns land as a burst: 3 reels within 20 minutes on 7 Oct ("Rituals of Joy" festive series) then a hero reel the next day.
- Format mix in the sample: roughly 2/3 carousels, 1/3 reels. Single images are rare. Reels sit in campaign clusters at the top of the grid.
- Almost every post is a **collab post** with the individual property account (aman + amansvetistefan), so the parent feed reads as one calm voice while each property gets reach.

## 2. Composition rules (what they actually do)

1. **Horizon low or high, never centred.** Sea shots put the waterline at roughly the top third (Sveti Stefan terrace, sea fills 60% of the frame) or the lower fifth (lone boat on glassy water, sky fills the rest). The centred horizon appears only in symmetrical reflection shots (villa reflected in a black infinity pool), where symmetry is the point.
2. **Negative space is a subject.** Typical frame: 50 to 70% sea, sky, mist, water or wall. One small focal object: a single fishing boat, two empty loungers, a lone umbrella, a flock of birds against dusk.
3. **People are small, turned away, or cropped.** Back of a woman meditating at a pool edge, a figure looking out under pine branches, a swimmer seated at the edge of an indoor pool. Nobody smiles at the camera. In fashion-led reels faces appear but are styled and unposed, never "happy guest" stock.
4. **Empty furniture as stand-in for the guest.** Two loungers under one parasol, a pair of deckchairs facing the sea, a set table before anyone sits. The viewer is invited into the empty seat.
5. **Frame within a frame.** Sunset through an open window, garden seen through timber louvres, sea seen through pine boughs with soft out-of-focus foreground leaves.
6. **Scale contrast.** Aerial top-down of rock, foam and terracotta roofs, then a hand-scale detail (stone wall with iron fixture and ivy). Big landscape followed by tactile close-up, always.
7. **Architecture shot square-on.** Verticals are corrected, facades and interiors frontal and symmetrical, lighting from practical lamps, no wide-angle distortion.
8. **Soft foreground blur.** A branch or leaf drifts into the top edge or corner (Sveti Stefan beach slide). It makes images feel observed rather than staged.

## 3. Colour grade

- **Warm but not orange.** Whites lean cream. Stone and sand read honey and biscuit. Skies are desaturated toward grey-blue or dusty rose rather than saturated cyan.
- **Saturation pulled back about 10 to 20%** except one hero hue per image (the Adriatic navy, a terracotta roof, a turquoise reef). Greens are muted toward olive.
- **Lifted, soft blacks; gentle highlight roll-off.** Shadows never crush to pure black except deliberate night interiors. Contrast is medium-low, mid-tones carry the image.
- **Film feel in reels and campaign work.** Visible grain, slight halation on highlights, softer focus, warmer and more muted than the stills (the "Rituals of Joy" reels look like 35mm or a film emulation). Stills are cleaner, digital, but share the warm and muted balance.
- **Golden-hour bias.** A large share is late afternoon or dusk light. Midday images appear only where the sea colour is the subject.
- **Mist and atmosphere.** Haze over mountains, fog on water, dusk gradients. Atmosphere is used to reduce detail and quieten the frame.

## 4. Recurring subjects

Water (sea, pools, reflections, still lakes), stone (walls, cliffs, temple ruins), timber louvres and screens, linen and draped fabric, candles and lamps, set tables, single boats, pine and olive branches, empty loungers, aerials of coastline, ritual gestures (hands, meditation, a dancer's arm, folded paper), local craft and culture (Korean madang, Japanese calligraphy). Rooms are shown as atmosphere, not as inventory: you rarely see a whole bedroom, you see light on a wall.

## 5. Graphic layer and typography

- **Mostly no text on images.** When there is text it is a small serif, centred, white on the image: a roman phrase with one italic word ("Setting the *Scene*", "Prelude *to* Joy", "Poised for the *Extraordinary*"). Series titles use wide-tracked caps with an italic lowercase connector ("RITUALS *of* JOY").
- The wordmark appears on landscape opener slides only, thin white caps over a mountain.
- **Carousel editorial card** (Sveti Stefan slide 2): a warm off-white field (approx. #F2EEE8) carries a 4-line serif paragraph top left, and a location line bottom left in small tracked caps ("AMAN SVETI STEFAN, MONTENEGRO"). The right half of the slide is the start of a panoramic aerial.
- **Panoramic stitching across slides.** One wide aerial is split across slides 2, 3 and 4 so swiping feels like a slow camera pan.
- **Inset photograph on off-white.** Slide 4 places a smaller portrait photo floating on the off-white field with generous margins, like a print on a page. Slide 6 does the same with a thin off-white border. This is the print-magazine device, and it is the most transferable idea to a website.

## 6. Caption voice

- Length: 280 to 800 characters, typically about 300 to 450. Two short paragraphs.
- Structure: (1) a sensory, seasonal opening sentence that names the place by @handle mid-sentence, (2) one or two sentences of rhythm of the day (mornings, afternoons, evenings), (3) a single soft line: "Discover more via the link in bio." That is the entire call to action.
- Vocabulary: softness, stillness, sanctity, rituals, unfolds, linger, summon, cocooning, tempo, tones of sky and sea, quiet magic. Seasons are named constantly (late summer, early autumn, the softness of autumn).
- Grammar: present tense, third person, no "you" commands, no exclamation marks, no emoji, almost no hashtags (one campaign tag such as #TheSpiritofAman on campaign reels only). Credits for DOP and locations go at the very end of campaign posts.
- Reel captions are shorter: 1 to 2 sentences plus a single line naming the location.
- What is never there: prices, rates, offers, "book now", urgency, superlatives about the brand itself, star ratings, guest testimonials, awards.

## 7. How they avoid the hard sell

- The product is time and atmosphere, not rooms. Captions describe how a day unfolds.
- Commerce lives one tap away (link in bio, collab profile), never in the frame.
- Each property gets the same voice and grade, so the brand sells consistency rather than any single deal.
- Seasonal framing creates a reason to post without a promotion.
- Comments are left to guests ("Oh so beautiful", "So relaxing!"). The brand does not chase engagement in captions.

# Photo direction rulebook for the Riviera property site

## A. Choosing listing photos

Pick, in this order of preference:
1. **Sea or garden seen from the property** with a low or high horizon and a strong empty area. Terrace edge, balustrade or pine branch in the foreground.
2. **Dusk or golden-hour exteriors** of the house with pool reflections. Symmetrical frontal facades.
3. **Empty seats facing a view**: two loungers, a table on a terrace, chairs under a pergola.
4. **Material details**: limestone, terracotta tiles, old shutters, linen, olive wood, wrought iron, bougainvillea on a wall.
5. **Interiors only when lit by daylight** with one window as the light source and few objects in frame.
6. **Aerials of the coastline** where the villa is small in the frame.

Reject:
- Wide-angle interiors with converging verticals or fisheye stretch.
- HDR look: haloes, grey skies pulled to cyan, glowing grass, crushed or flat shadows.
- Bright midday shots with harsh shadows, unless sea colour is the point.
- Visible cars, bins, pool robots, TVs, branded objects, people facing camera, agency watermarks.
- Bathrooms and kitchens as lead images (allowed deep in a gallery, max one each).
- Twilight shots with every lamp blazing and the sky pushed electric blue.

Target per listing: 8 to 14 images. At least one aerial or long view, one dusk image, two material details, one empty-seat image.

## B. Cropping

- Hero (desktop): 16:9 or 21:9 full bleed, horizon on the upper third or lower fifth, subject off-centre. Mobile hero: 4:5, recropped (not squashed) with `object-position` set per image.
- Gallery: mix of 4:5 portrait and 3:2 landscape. Never square except for a detail inset.
- Detail insets: 4:5, displayed smaller and floating on the off-white page with generous margins (the Aman inset device). Margin around an inset at least 8vw on desktop.
- Leave headroom: crop sea and sky in, not out. If in doubt, give the empty part of the frame more room.
- Straighten every horizon to 0 degrees and correct verticals before anything else.
- Store a focal point per image in data (`focal: [x, y]` in percent) and drive `object-position` from it so crops survive every breakpoint.

## C. Grade

Goal: warm whites, muted greens, soft blacks, one hero hue. Apply once in the build pipeline so every source photo from different agencies looks like one shoot.

### Sharp pipeline (preferred, baked at build)

```js
// scripts/grade.mjs  (sharp >= 0.33)
import sharp from "sharp";

export async function grade(input, output, { width = 2400 } = {}) {
  await sharp(input)
    .rotate()                          // respect EXIF
    .resize({ width, withoutEnlargement: true })
    .modulate({ brightness: 1.03, saturation: 0.86, hue: -3 }) // slightly warmer, quieter
    .linear(0.92, 14)                  // lower contrast, lift blacks (~14/255 floor)
    .recomb([                          // gentle warm split: cream highlights, olive greens
      [1.03, 0.02, -0.01],
      [0.01, 0.99,  0.00],
      [-0.02, 0.01, 0.95],
    ])
    .gamma(1.05)                       // soften mid-tone contrast
    .sharpen({ sigma: 0.5, m1: 0.3, m2: 0.6 }) // restrained, avoids crunchy agency sharpening
    .toFormat("avif", { quality: 58 })
    .toFile(output);
}
```

Per-image overrides (keep in the listing data, not in code):
- Over-saturated agency HDR: `saturation: 0.75`, `linear(0.88, 18)`.
- Blue-cast twilight: `hue: -8`, blue row of recomb `0.92`.
- Already warm golden-hour: skip `recomb`, keep `saturation: 0.92`.

Optional film layer for hero and video only: a 3 to 4% opacity monochrome grain PNG tile overlaid in CSS (below), never baked into gallery images.

### CSS fallback (when an image cannot be reprocessed)

```css
:root {
  --grade: saturate(.86) contrast(.92) brightness(1.03) sepia(.06);
  --grade-strong: saturate(.76) contrast(.88) brightness(1.04) sepia(.08);
}
.photo { filter: var(--grade); }
.photo--hdr { filter: var(--grade-strong); }

/* film grain, hero only */
.hero::after {
  content: ""; position: absolute; inset: 0; pointer-events: none;
  background: url("/grain.png"); opacity: .035; mix-blend-mode: multiply;
}
```

`sepia(.06)` is the cream warmth. Never exceed `sepia(.1)`: past that it turns nostalgic and brown.

### Page colour that the photos sit on

Off-white field sampled by eye from the Aman carousel: about `#F2EEE8`. Pair with a slightly darker sand for blocks (about `#E8E1D6`) and charcoal type (about `#2B2925`), not black. Photos should always meet this field with no border, no shadow, no rounded corners.

## D. Sequencing (property page)

Follow the Aman carousel rhythm: wide, words, pan, detail, life, texture, close.

1. **Opening**: the long view. Sea or landscape, property small or partly visible. Full bleed, no overlay text except the property name in small tracked caps.
2. **Text field**: off-white panel with 2 to 4 lines of serif prose and a location line in tracked caps ("CAP FERRAT, ALPES-MARITIMES"). Optionally the next photo bleeds in from the right edge.
3. **Pan**: one wide aerial or panorama shown across a horizontally scrolling strip, or two adjacent frames cropped from the same image, so scrolling feels like a camera move.
4. **Inset detail**: a 4:5 material close-up floating on off-white with wide margins.
5. **Life without people**: empty loungers, a laid table, an open shutter.
6. **Texture**: stone, tile, linen, iron.
7. **Close**: dusk. Pool reflection or sea at last light. Then the facts (price, surface, bedrooms) set quietly in small type below, after the images, never on top of them.

Rhythm rules across the whole site:
- Alternate scale: never two aerials or two details in a row.
- Alternate light: day, day, dusk is fine; never three dusk images in a row.
- One human figure per page at most, small and turned away.
- Every listing card on the index uses its opening image graded identically, so the index reads as one series, the way the Aman grid does.

## E. Words on and around photos

- Default: no text on images.
- Allowed overlay: one short serif phrase with one italic word, centred, white, set small (clamp 18 to 28px). Example patterns for the Riviera: "The *Long* Afternoon", "Before the *Mistral*", "Light *on* Stone".
- Captions under images: one line, place and season ("Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat, late September"), small tracked caps or small italic serif.
- Property descriptions follow the Aman caption shape: one sensory sentence about place and season, one about how a day unfolds there, one quiet line to enquire. 300 to 450 characters. No exclamation marks, no superlatives, no "stunning", no "exclusive opportunity". Never use em dashes or en dashes.
- Enquiry line, Aman style: "Request the particulars." or "Arrange a private viewing." Nothing louder.

## F. Motion (if video or Ken Burns is used)

- Slow: 8 to 12 seconds per shot, scale change no more than 4% over the shot.
- Cross-dissolves of 1.2 to 1.6 seconds. No whip pans, no zoom punches, no parallax gimmicks.
- Ambient sound only (sea, cicadas, wind in pines) if any audio at all.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: show the still.
